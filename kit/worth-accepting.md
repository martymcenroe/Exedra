# What makes a scenario worth accepting

The acceptance gate decides whether a pack can be published. It cannot decide whether the day is
worth playing. A pack can pass every check, with its optimum replayed to the penny, and still be a
morning in which the right move is obvious from the first turn. Nobody learns anything from that day, and the
venue learns nothing from watching people play it.

## It probes something

Palaestra exists to find standards gaps by experiment. A scenario belongs on the shelf when playing
it surfaces one of two things:

- an obligation two reasonable operators would read differently, an ambiguity a player can often
  exploit;
- a rule that produces a perverse outcome when someone follows it exactly.

Say which one your scenario probes, in a sentence, before you build it. A team that cannot say what
its scenario probes has not finished designing it.

## Its two metrics pull against each other

Every pack scores operating cost and reliability contribution. A good day makes them disagree: the
cheapest play leaves relief undelivered, or full relief costs real money. If one play wins on both
from the start, nothing on the day asks the player to decide anything.

## The player has a real choice at a real moment

The interesting turn is the one where the player commits before the information arrives. The
battery is filled at dawn for a peak that may not come, or relief is offered before the price is
known. Build that turn into the day, and give the player something on the screen to reason from.

## The brief tells the player what they would know

`brief_md` is the page a player reads before pressing start. It says what the operator in that seat
would know that morning, from what the board shows, its forecast window included, and nothing the
day itself is meant to reveal. The schema requires a brief, so write a working one before the first
check. Rewrite it after you have played the day, then check the final file again.

## The numbers say where they came from

A compiled day's numbers come from its source, and its provenance says how to reproduce them. A
hand-authored day says so and is dedicated CC0. A plausible invented day is welcome. A day that
presents invented numbers as a real one is not.

## What to write down

When you submit, include a short entry with the pack:

- **The seat:** the operator the player stands in for.
- **The tension:** the two outcomes the day sets against each other.
- **The control points:** what the player can actually move.
- **What it teaches:** what a player understands after the day that they did not before.
- **What it probes:** the obligation, rule or ambiguity, from the list at the top.
